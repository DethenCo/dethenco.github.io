Our research group spent three days in Philadelphia for Breakthrough T1D's Clinical & Research Congress (CRC2026), an inaugural meeting dedicated entirely to type 1 diabetes, spanning translational and clinical research. The congress brought the T1D research community together to take stock of recent advances and their real-world impact on people living with the disease, and it made for a genuinely rewarding few days of discussion and exchange.

## Introducing DeThenCo

For the first time, the team also introduced DeThenCo - Decoding the Non-Coding - to the T1D Fund, presenting the spin-off project at this early, formative stage of its development.

It was a great meeting, with great people and great discussion, and a strong reminder of how far the T1D research community has come, and how much of that progress is starting to reach the people it's meant to help.


## Predicting the onset of clinical type 1 diabetes

Erika Pedace presented her work on predicting the onset of Stage 3 type 1 diabetes using circulating small RNAs, tracing their origin back to neutrophils; innate immune cells with a complex and still-underexplored role in T1D development.

## The DISCO-T1D project

The team also presented several e-posters reporting progress on DISCO-T1D, a project fully funded by a Career Development Award from Breakthrough T1D. Led by Daniela Fignani together with Ludovica Lopresti, Elena Aiello, and Giada Licata, the project aims to improve the technology and bioinformatic data analysis - led by Mohamad Elrifai - used to detect and measure non-coding RNAs in blood as biomarkers for type 1 diabetes and for response to immunotherapies.

## Genetic variants shape circulating miRNA levels in type 1 diabetes

In the first miRNA expression quantitative trait loci (miR-eQTL) analysis in people with type 1 diabetes, the team mapped genetic variants that control plasma miRNA levels across two independent INNODIA cohorts (111 and 144 participants). Fifteen miR-eQTLs were found in both cohorts, and four of them sit within type 1 diabetes risk loci but are driven by different causal variants. One variant linked to miR-101-3p was also associated with the rate of beta-cell function decline. The results show that a substantial part of the variability in circulating miRNAs is inherited and separable from type 1 diabetes risk, which matters for their use as disease biomarkers.

## Synthetic data confirms a 14q32 miRNA signature for stratifying type 1 diabetes

The team used synthetic data augmentation (TVAE) to expand a cohort of 115 people with recent-onset type 1 diabetes and retest the 14q32 miRNA signature that separates two molecular subgroups of patients. At 315 samples, the augmented dataset reproduced the original subgroups and signature, and the resulting classifier was validated on an independent cohort of 147 people. Pushing augmentation further, to 1,115 samples, kept accuracy high but diluted the underlying biology, showing that synthetic data needs careful calibration.

## Protecting beta cells with a tardigrade protein

Giuseppina Emanuela Grieco and Silvia Cantara presented a novel approach to protecting pancreatic beta cells in type 1 diabetes using the tardigrade DSUP protein, best known for its role in protecting DNA from stress and damage.
