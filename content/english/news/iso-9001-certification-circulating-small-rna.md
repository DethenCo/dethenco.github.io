Our research group has obtained International Quality Management System certification (ISO 9001) for the analysis of circulating small RNAs using sequencing and PCR technologies. It's a basic requirement on paper, but a foundational one for what we're building toward. Here's why it matters.

## Reproducibility

Circulating small RNAs are low-abundance molecules, and much of the variability reported in the literature comes down to pre-analytical and analytical factors — hemolysis, platelet contamination, tube type, time to processing, freeze–thaw cycles, extraction chemistry, spike-ins, and bioinformatic analysis. We've spent years standardizing the entire pipeline; under a certified quality management system, every one of those steps is now controlled and documented at the point of execution.

## Traceability

Every sample, reagent lot, instrument, calibration, operator, and software version is documented, so any result can be traced back to exactly how it was obtained. Deviations are recorded, investigated, and closed. When a small RNA signature does - or doesn't - replicate, we can ask why, and answer with evidence.

## Biomarker consistency

A circulating small RNA signature is only useful if it can travel: from discovery to validation cohorts, to other laboratories, and eventually to a clinical setting. Standardized, auditable processes make our data usable by academic collaborators, consortium partners, clinicians, and industry , and they're the prerequisite for everything that comes after: multi-centre validation, accreditation, regulatory pathways.

## A different way of working

Beyond the data itself, certification changes how an academic research lab thinks and analyzes. Internal audits, non-conformity management, corrective actions, and measurable quality objectives keep us honest about what worked — and make sure it works again next time. Continuous quality improvement is the rule that makes a biomarker credible, and real.

This is the first step. The real work starts now.
